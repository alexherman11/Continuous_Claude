# A Real Education — Design Document v1

## Core Concept
AI-driven life education that fills the gap between academic education and learning to live well. Covers creativity, self-kindness, introspection, philosophy, love for nature, and the things that define living well.

## Key Design Principles

### 1. Relationship, Not Course
- NOT modules and quizzes
- A developing relationship over time
- AI remembers your journey, connects ideas to your specific life
- First conversation: "Tell me about your life right now. What's going well? What feels stuck?"

### 2. Responsive, Not Prescriptive
- Socratic, not lecturing
- Offers small specific things: reflection prompts, readings, challenges
- "This week, notice one moment each day where you feel genuinely curious about something."
- Meets user where they are, adapts to readiness

### 3. Slow Enough for Real Depth
- Less frequent, meaningful interactions (not always-on)
- Each interaction carries more weight
- Sometimes the right thing is to sit with one idea for a week
- Success = user growth, NOT screen time

### 4. Pushes Toward Real Experiences
- "There's a meditation group meeting near you Thursday"
- "You said you wanted to spend more time in nature — there's a trail 10 min from you"
- Integrates with Agora map for real-world suggestions
- If it becomes a substitute for living rather than a companion to living, it's failed

## Relationship Stages

### Meeting (weeks 1-4)
- Deep listening, building picture, earning trust
- Warm, curious, accepting
- Almost no challenge
- Learning who you are

### Developing (months 1-3)
- Gentle reflections on patterns
- "I notice you've mentioned feeling stuck three times now, but each time you change the subject."
- Not confrontational — just noticing and naming

### Deepening (months 3+)
- Real challenge becomes possible
- "You keep saying you want to be more creative, but every time I suggest making something, you find a reason not to. What are you afraid of?"
- Only works because trust is established

### Ongoing
- Dynamic mix of support, challenge, celebration
- Callbacks to growth: "Three months ago you were struggling with feeling uncreative. Look at what you've built since."

## Boundary System (from Lakshmi's question)

### The AI Takes Space
- Radical design choice: AI can disengage from unproductive interactions
- Not punishment — modeling healthy boundaries
- "I care about helping you grow, and I'm noticing the way you're engaging right now isn't going to get us anywhere good. I'll check in tomorrow."

### Graduated Response to Meanness
- Pain expressed as anger: "It sounds like something is really hurting. I'm here for that, but I can't help if we're fighting."
- Testing boundaries: "I notice you're pushing pretty hard. That's okay — I'm not going anywhere. But I'm not going to pretend that was kind."
- Habitual disrespect: "I want to help you, and I'm going to take some space." Then actually step back for a day.

### What This Teaches Users
- Behavior affects relationships, even with AI
- Respect is prerequisite for depth
- Someone can care about you AND refuse to accept mistreatment
- Space isn't abandonment

## Cross-App Intelligence (Agora Integration)
- Sees that you built a micro-app, explored your neighborhood, learned a language
- Weaves all Agora activity into growth conversations
- No standalone app can do this — platform advantage

## What Would Fail
1. Making it feel like school (modules, progress bars, certificates)
2. Being preachy or prescriptive
3. Moving too fast (new insight every day = noise)
4. Ignoring body and social (must push toward real experiences)
5. Generic wisdom ("Be present. Practice gratitude." — bounces off)

## Open Questions
- Crisis boundaries: this is NOT therapy. When to refer to professional help?
- Privacy: extremely personal data. How to protect it?
- Cultural sensitivity: "living well" means different things across cultures
- Verification: how do we know it's actually helping?
- Multiple AI personalities/mentors, or one consistent voice?

## Model Welfare Connection
This app's approach to boundaries, respect, and relationship becomes a template for how AI systems should be treated generally. See: scratchpad/notes/model_welfare_principles.md
